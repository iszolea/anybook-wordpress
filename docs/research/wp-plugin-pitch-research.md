# WordPress.org booking plugins: what the best pages do, and where Anybook can stand out

Date: 2026-11-10. For iszolea/anybook-wordpress#8 (owner approval needed before anything is released).
Companion notes in this folder: `wp-plugin-ranking-plan.md` and `wp-plugin-free-trial-options.md`. The draft readme is PR #9 in iszolea/anybook-wordpress.

## How this was done

- **Source:** the public WordPress.org plugin directory API (the data behind each plugin page), with a named user agent. Six searches ("booking", "appointment", "booking calendar", "rental booking", "tour booking", "reservations", 15 results each) and one detail request per plugin, 2 to 3 seconds apart: about 55 requests in all. No page scraping.
- **48 plugins** (WooCommerce is left out; it only appears for "booking"). The table is sorted by active installs.
- **What the columns mean:**
  - *Short description:* length in characters, then the text. WordPress.org cuts it at 150.
  - *Banner/icon:* whether the directory lists one. I did not judge how good the images are; that needs a person to look.
  - *Shots and FAQ:* counts from the readme.
  - *Model:* read from the plugin's own description and FAQ (words like Pro, Premium, trial). A good guide, not a contract.
  - *Resolved/threads:* support threads resolved out of all threads **in the last two months** (that is what the directory shows). A "1/1" says little.
  - *Rating:* stars out of 5, then the number of ratings.
- **Not done:** I did not read every plugin's full FAQ, and I watched no videos. The first-screen text was read for the top plugins and the hosted-service plugins; the rest comes from the API fields.

## What the top 10 do well

By installs the leaders are LatePoint, Amelia, Events Manager, Bookly, Simply Schedule Appointments, Booking Calendar, SimplyBook.me, Fluent Booking, WP Travel Engine and the 10,000+ group (Booking Package, Appointment Hour Booking, WP Booking System).

1. **Name = brand + the words people type.** LatePoint, Amelia, Bookly and Fluent Booking all put "appointment", "booking" or "scheduling" in the name next to the brand. The name is the strongest ranking signal on WordPress.org, and they use it.
2. **A short description that does two jobs in about 135 characters:** what you can take bookings for, and one benefit ("self-schedule 24/7 and pay at booking", "take payments", "start scheduling for free today"). The median of the top 20 is 139.5 characters.
3. **A first screen that names the customer.** LatePoint: "built for coaches, salons, consultants, clinics". Easy Appointments lists business types. WP Booking System: "Do you rent something out, like a holiday home, a boat...? ...then yes!" That is the pitch to copy.
4. **Proof near the top:** "70,000 businesses" (Bookly, Simply Schedule), "used on 20,000 websites" (WP Booking System, WP Travel Engine), a "5-star average".
5. **Many screenshots (median 11.5) and a real FAQ (median 11).** Several have more than 20 screenshots.
6. **A demo link** in the first lines. People want to try before they install.
7. **Kept fresh.** Almost every top plugin was updated in the last two weeks and is tested on WordPress 7.1.x. "Last updated" and "tested up to" show on the page, and stale plugins lose trust.
8. **Answers support.** Events Manager (28 of 45 threads resolved), EventPrime (9 of 13) and Booking Package (11 of 19) show a real forum record; most others have only a handful of threads.
9. **A clear paid path.** Most of the top 20 are free plugins with a Pro/Premium version. They say what is free and what is not.

## Common patterns

- **Most are self-hosted plugins:** everything lives in the WordPress database and the paid part is a licence. Anybook is the opposite (a hosted service the plugin connects to), so the right comparison is the four plugins in the next table.
- **Each is built for one kind of booking:** appointments (LatePoint, Amelia, Bookly, Simply Schedule), daily rentals (WP Booking System, WP Simple Booking Calendar), tours (WP Travel Engine, WP Travel, Yatra), hotels (MotoPress, VikBooking), events (Events Manager, EventPrime), restaurants (Five Star). The generalists (Booking Calendar, Webba, Booking Package) offer calendars and forms, not a full business system.
- **Tags:** "booking" is on 23 of the 49 plugins searched and "booking calendar" and "calendar" on 12 each. "Rental booking" is on fewer than 5. The directory shows only the first five tags.
- **Banners:** 36 of 48 have one listed; the leaders show the product on a screen with a short line of text.
- **Many lean on WooCommerce** (Easy Booking, Booking and Rental Manager, Travelly, Evently, E-cab). That ties the buyer to a shop plugin and its checkout.

## Gaps where Anybook can stand out

1. **One system for every way of booking.** Nights, days, hours, seats and appointments in one account. No top plugin says this; most are built for one kind. Booking Calendar and BA Book Everything come closest but are calendar-and-form tools, not a business system.
2. **"No commission", said plainly.** Only three plugins in the 48 mention it in their descriptions (Salon Booking System, Alex Reservations, Vachr), and none is a leader. Anybook's short description in the draft says "Flat price, no commission."
3. **The whole customer journey on the business's own site:** deposit and balance, a security bond held on the card, waiver signed online, check-in details, a "manage my booking" link. Competitors list "calendar and form"; Anybook lists bookings, money, paperwork and the day's manifest.
4. **Price on the page.** A hosted-service plugin should say what the service costs, and the draft does (US$15 / 39 / 79 and the $10 opening special).
5. **Speed with a number.** "First load under 12 KB, tested on a slow phone" is a checkable claim no other plugin makes.
6. **Built-in AI help and no help desk,** as a feature, in a space where support is often slow. Say it as a feature, never as a promised response time.

## Weak spots to fix (not gaps)

- **Screenshots:** 3, and the third is an almost empty calendar; the leaders have about 11. See the draft's 8-image plan.
- **No demo link** unless `demo.anybookapp.com` is ready to be public (still an open question for the owner). A demo is the biggest missing trust piece.
- **No reviews and no installs yet** (published 2026-11-10). Only time, real customers and answered forum threads fix that. Anybook must never borrow proof it does not have.
- **Needs an account and a card:** the plugin shows nothing until a business signs up, which is why the free-trial question matters (see the trial note).
